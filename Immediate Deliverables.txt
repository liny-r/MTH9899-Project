MTH9899 Final Project Intermediate deliverables 
Prediction problem description 
1. The purpose of this project is to build a machine learning model to predict the next-day residual stock returns at 15:30 for a universe of approximately 500 stocks, trained on 5 years of data from 2010 to 2014. 
Target variable
1. Make predictions at 15:30 for the return over the next 24 hrs i.e. use the next day’s cumulative residual return at 15:30, combined with the return from 15:30 to 16:00 ”today” as your target variable. Consider clipping your target variable prior to fitting.
Data Source
1. Daily data
2. Intraday data 
Data Cleaning and Preparation
1. Prepare cumulative residual return target variable by adding returns 15:30-16:00 today with 16:00 today - 15:30 tomorrow. Clip actual returns at 5 MAD cross-sectionally. Normalise returns with EST_VOL before fitting. 
2. Data cleaning, adjust prices and shares using adjustment factors 
3. Prepare validation data using only the first 4 years 
Proposed Technical Features List
Highlighted = include in intermediate deliverable
* All features will be computed using information available up to 15:30 on each trading day.
1. Daily Data
   1. Risk-adjusted features
      1. Volatility-adjusted return: normalize intraday return by volatility (return / EST_VOL) to ensure returns are scared by risk 
   2. Daily time-series features - momentum/ reversal
      1. Short term momentum/reversal (1-day) - previous day return to capture short-term momentum/reversal across days 
      2. Medium term momentum - previous 21 day returns to capture short-term momentum across days 
      3. Momentum acceleration - Difference between 5-day return and 21-day return (recent speed vs. slower trend) to capture whether momentum is accelerating or decelerating.
      4. Return deviation from moving average (return - MA20)/MA20. Stocks far from their average often revert. 
   3. Liquidity/ Firm characteristics
      1. Liquidity - log(MDV63) to distinguish more liquid stocks returns that are more predictable 
      2. Free float percentage: stocks with smaller float may experience stronger price moves. 
   4. Volatility signals
      1. Compute 10-day realized daily return volatility (annualized), divide by EST_VOL. When realized vol is much lower than estimated vol, the stock may be "coiling" before a move; high ratio may signal mean-reversion.
   5. Volume signals
      1. Dollar volume trend: ratio of 5-day average volume to MDV_63. Expanding volume relative to recent history may signal building interest.
2. Intraday Data
1. Intraday time-series features
   1. Overnight return: CumReturnResid at the first intraday snapshot (9:45), which captures return from prior day's 16:00 through 9:45. Overnight gap may predict next-day continuation or reversal 
   2. Short-term Intraday momentum in the last hour (returns from 14:30 - 15:30): Stocks with strong recent momentum may continue trending. 
   3. Today’s cumulative return from 9:45 - 15:30 captures total intraday movement thus far. Daily strength often persists overnight. 
   4. Morning momentum (returns from 9:45 - 12:00): Captures early-day market sentiment
   5. Intraday momentum at 15:30: CumReturnResid at 15:30 minus CumReturnResid at 12:00 (afternoon drift) because late-day price pressure often persists into the next day 
   6. Intraday reversal - difference between early day return (returns from 9:45 - 12:00) and afternoon return (returns from 12:00 - 15:30) captures potential mean reversion behaviour
2. Volatility signals
   1. Intraday volatility - standard deviation of intraday returns during the day up to 15:30 to capture intraday volatility.
3. Volume signals 
   1. Volume surprise - cumulative volume today until 15:30 / MDV63 to detect unusual trading activity like informed trading or news 
   2. Intraday volume acceleration: CumVolume at 15:30 divided by CumVolume at 12:00. If volume is picking up significantly in the afternoon, it signals conviction in the move
   3. Late day volume spike: (volume 14:30-15:30 / volume 9:45 - 14:30) Large late-date volume may signal price pressure continuing into the close. 
   4. Return-volume correlation (intraday): Compute the correlation between the 15-minute return increments and volume increments throughout the day. Price moves accompanied by rising volume are more likely to be "real" signals.
4. Cross-sectional features 
   1. Cross-sectional relative return rank: cross-sectional rank of today’s return across stocks 
   2. Cross-sectional relative volume rank: cross-sectional rank of today’s volume / MDV_63 across stocks 
Text Features 
1. Earnings call transcripts 
   1. Sentiment score: a positive sentiment score is expected to have a likely positive impact on returns. Score the tone of the earning call transcripts using the Loughran–McDonald financial sentiment dictionary.
   2. Change in sentiment: Compare sentiment scores between from the previous call and the current call. Improving sentiment may predict positive returns. 
   3. Earnings surprise language: Certain languages may indicate unexpected strong/weak performance. 
   4. Management confidence and outlook: measure frequency of words like “growth”, “strong”, “opportunity”, “expansion”
   5. Management uncertainty score: measure frequency of words like “uncertain”, “difficult”, “challenging” and "unpredictable", often predicts higher volatility and negative drift.
   6. Innovation and growth score: measure frequency of worlds like “innovation”, “technology”, “R&D” 
2. 10-Q filing 
   1. Risk disclosure frequency: count words related to risk in 10-Q filings. This gives insight into the company’s risk management attitude and approaches or their current risk discussions. 
   2. 10-Q filing change score: Compute the cosine similarity between the current quarter's 10-Q risk factors section and the prior quarter's
Model choices
1. Regularized OLS - ridge regression
2. Random Forest Regressor
3. XGBoost

Section 1: Daily Data Features
1.1 Risk-Adjusted Features
Feature
	Definition
	Formula / Pseudocode
	Normalization
	Motivation
	Volatility-Adjusted Return
	Intraday residual return scaled by the stock's annualized volatility estimate, so that returns are expressed in units of risk.
	feat = CumReturnResid_1530 / EST_VOL
	Winsorize at ±5 cross-sectionally per date, then z-score cross-sectionally within each date.
	Scaling by volatility puts all stocks on a comparable risk-adjusted basis. A large raw return from a low-vol stock carries more information than the same return from a high-vol stock.
	 
1.2 Momentum & Reversal Features
Feature
	Definition
	Formula / Pseudocode
	Normalization
	Motivation
	Short-Term Reversal (1-day)
	Prior day's adjusted close-to-close return. Captures the well-known 1-day reversal effect in equities.
	ret_1d = (Close_{t-1} * PxAdjFactor_{t-1}) / (Close_{t-2} * PxAdjFactor_{t-2}) - 1
	Winsorize at ±5 MAD cross-sectionally per date. Z-score cross-sectionally within each date.
	Short-term return reversal is one of the most robust anomalies: yesterday's losers tend to outperform and vice versa, driven by market microstructure and liquidity provision.
	Medium-Term Momentum (5d & 21d)
	Cumulative adjusted return over the past 5 or 21 trading days, skipping day t-1 to avoid reversal contamination.
	ret_5d = (Close_{t-2} * PxAdj_{t-2}) / (Close_{t-7} * PxAdj_{t-7}) - 1 ret_21d = (Close_{t-2} * PxAdj_{t-2}) / (Close_{t-23} * PxAdj_{t-23}) - 1
	Winsorize each at ±5 MAD cross-sectionally. Z-score cross-sectionally within each date.
	Medium-term momentum (1-12 months) is a classic factor. Recent winners tend to continue outperforming over the next month, likely due to delayed investor reaction to news.
	Momentum Acceleration
	Difference between the 5-day return and the 21-day return. Measures whether recent momentum is speeding up or slowing down relative to the longer-run trend.
	mom_accel = ret_5d - ret_21d
	Winsorize at ±5 MAD cross-sectionally. Z-score cross-sectionally within each date.
	A stock with accelerating momentum (5d >> 21d) may signal a fresh catalyst; decelerating momentum may signal exhaustion. This captures a non-linear aspect not present in either return alone.
	Return Deviation from Moving Average
	Distance of today's close from its 20-day moving average, normalized by the moving average. Captures how far the stock has strayed from its recent trend.
	ma20 = mean(Close_{t-1} ... Close_{t-20}, adjusted) feat = (Close_{t-1,adj} - ma20) / ma20
	Winsorize at ±5 MAD cross-sectionally. Z-score cross-sectionally within each date.
	Stocks far above their moving average tend to mean-revert; stocks far below may also revert. This is a widely used technical signal for identifying stretched valuations relative to recent history.
	 
1.3 Liquidity & Firm Characteristics
Feature
	Definition
	Formula / Pseudocode
	Normalization
	Motivation
	Log Liquidity (MDV_63)
	Natural log of the median daily dollar volume over the past 63 trading days. Captures the size/liquidity tier of the stock.
	feat = log(MDV_63)
	No winsorization needed (log compresses scale). Z-score cross-sectionally within each date.
	More liquid stocks have more efficient prices and different return dynamics than illiquid ones. Including log(MDV_63) lets the model adjust predictions based on liquidity regime.
	Free Float Percentage
	The fraction of shares freely available for public trading. Low-float stocks have fewer shares available and can experience amplified price moves.
	feat = FREE_FLOAT_PERCENTAGE (raw field)
	Winsorize at [1st, 99th] percentile cross-sectionally. Z-score cross-sectionally within each date.
	Stocks with a smaller free float have less liquidity buffer against buying/selling pressure, making their returns more volatile and potentially more predictable from volume signals.
	 
1.4 Volatility Signals
Feature
	Definition
	Formula / Pseudocode
	Normalization
	Motivation
	Realized Vol / EST_VOL Ratio
	Ratio of the stock's 10-day realized daily return volatility (annualized) to EST_VOL. Measures whether realized risk is below or above the model's estimate.
	realized_vol = std(daily_rets last 10d) * sqrt(252) feat = realized_vol / EST_VOL
	Winsorize at [1st, 99th] percentile cross-sectionally. Z-score cross-sectionally within each date.
	When realized vol is much lower than EST_VOL the stock may be 'coiling' before a breakout. A high ratio may signal mean-reversion as extreme moves normalize. Both regimes may have return predictability.
	 
1.5 Volume Signals
Feature
	Definition
	Formula / Pseudocode
	Normalization
	Motivation
	Dollar Volume Trend
	Ratio of the 5-day average daily dollar volume to MDV_63. Detects whether recent trading interest is expanding or contracting relative to the 3-month baseline.
	avg_vol_5d = mean(Volume_{t-1}...Volume_{t-5} * Close_adj) feat = avg_vol_5d / MDV_63
	Winsorize at ±5 MAD cross-sectionally. Z-score cross-sectionally within each date.
	Expanding volume relative to the 3-month average may signal growing investor interest or an emerging news catalyst, both of which can precede a persistent price move.
	 
Section 2: Intraday Data Features
2.1 Intraday Time-Series Features
Feature
	Definition
	Formula / Pseudocode
	Normalization
	Motivation
	Overnight Return
	CumReturnResid at the first intraday snapshot (9:45), capturing the residual return from the prior day's 16:00 close through 9:45 AM.
	feat = CumReturnResid at time 09:45
	Winsorize at ±5 MAD cross-sectionally. Z-score cross-sectionally within each date.
	Overnight gaps reflect after-hours news and pre-market sentiment. Large overnight moves may predict continuation (news drift) or reversal (overreaction), making this a strong predictor.
	Short-Term Intraday Momentum (14:30-15:30)
	Return during the last hour before prediction time. Captures very recent price pressure heading into the close.
	feat = CumReturnResid_1530 - CumReturnResid_1430
	Winsorize at ±5 MAD cross-sectionally. Z-score cross-sectionally within each date.
	Strong momentum in the last hour often reflects end-of-day institutional order flow or positioning. This near-term pressure frequently persists into the next trading session.
	Total Intraday Return (9:45-15:30)
	Cumulative residual return from market open through the prediction window at 15:30. Captures the full intraday directional move.
	feat = CumReturnResid_1530 - CumReturnResid_0945
	Winsorize at ±5 MAD cross-sectionally. Z-score cross-sectionally within each date.
	A strong full-day move often reflects a fundamental catalyst that can continue into the next day (momentum) or has already been fully priced in (reversal). Interaction with volume distinguishes the two.
	Morning Momentum (9:45-12:00)
	Residual return during the morning session only. Captures how the stock behaved in the first half of the day before afternoon dynamics set in.
	feat = CumReturnResid_1200 - CumReturnResid_0945
	Winsorize at ±5 MAD cross-sectionally. Z-score cross-sectionally within each date.
	Morning returns reflect opening sentiment and early reaction to news. A strong morning that fades suggests reversal pressure; a slow morning that accelerates in the afternoon signals conviction.
	Afternoon Drift (12:00-15:30)
	Residual return during the afternoon session only. Captures late-day directional price pressure distinct from the morning move.
	feat = CumReturnResid_1530 - CumReturnResid_1200
	Winsorize at ±5 MAD cross-sectionally. Z-score cross-sectionally within each date.
	Late-day price pressure is associated with institutional portfolio rebalancing and often persists into the next day. Separating afternoon from morning isolates this effect.
	Intraday Reversal (Morning minus Afternoon)
	Difference between the morning return and the afternoon return. A positive value means the stock surged in the morning but gave back gains in the afternoon.
	feat = (CumReturnResid_1200 - CumReturnResid_0945) - (CumReturnResid_1530 - CumReturnResid_1200)
	Winsorize at ±5 MAD cross-sectionally. Z-score cross-sectionally within each date.
	When a strong morning move reverses in the afternoon, it often signals the initial move was an overreaction. This pattern tends to continue into the next day, creating a short-term mean-reversion signal.
	 
2.2 Intraday Volatility Signals
Feature
	Definition
	Formula / Pseudocode
	Normalization
	Motivation
	Intraday Volatility
	Standard deviation of the 15-minute interval return increments from 9:45 to 15:30 (i.e., the standard deviation of the per-interval changes in CumReturnResid).
	intervals = [CumReturnResid_t - CumReturnResid_{t-1} for each 15-min step] feat = std(intervals)
	Winsorize at [1st, 99th] percentile cross-sectionally. Z-score cross-sectionally within each date.
	High intraday volatility may indicate news uncertainty or an active trading catalyst. Combined with directional signals, it helps distinguish noisy noise from informed moves.
	 
2.3 Intraday Volume Signals
Feature
	Definition
	Formula / Pseudocode
	Normalization
	Motivation
	Volume Surprise
	Ratio of cumulative volume from 9:45 to 15:30 today to MDV_63 (the 3-month average daily volume baseline). Detects unusual trading activity.
	feat = CumVolume_1530 / MDV_63
	Winsorize at [1st, 99th] percentile cross-sectionally. Z-score cross-sectionally within each date.
	Abnormally high volume relative to the historical baseline signals informed trading, a news catalyst, or index rebalancing. High-volume moves are more likely to be 'real' and to persist.
	Intraday Volume Acceleration
	Ratio of cumulative volume at 15:30 to cumulative volume at 12:00. Captures whether trading activity is picking up or slowing in the second half of the day.
	feat = CumVolume_1530 / CumVolume_1200
	Winsorize at [1st, 99th] percentile cross-sectionally. Z-score cross-sectionally within each date.
	Accelerating afternoon volume signals rising conviction in a directional move and is often associated with institutional order flow. A price move backed by accelerating volume is more likely to persist overnight.
	Late-Day Volume Spike
	Ratio of volume in the last hour (14:30-15:30) to the volume in the earlier part of the day (9:45-14:30). Detects a spike in activity close to the prediction time.
	vol_late = CumVolume_1530 - CumVolume_1430 vol_early = CumVolume_1430 - CumVolume_0945 feat = vol_late / vol_early
	Winsorize at [1st, 99th] percentile cross-sectionally. Z-score cross-sectionally within each date.
	A large late-day volume spike relative to the earlier session indicates unusual end-of-day positioning, which can create price pressure that continues through the close and into the next morning.
	Return-Volume Correlation (Intraday)
	Pearson correlation between the 15-minute return increments and 15-minute volume increments throughout the day, computed per stock per day using all intervals up to 15:30.
	ret_inc = diff(CumReturnResid) vol_inc = diff(CumVolume) feat = corr(ret_inc, vol_inc)
	No winsorization (correlation is bounded [-1,1]). Z-score cross-sectionally within each date.
	When price moves occur alongside increasing volume, they are more likely to reflect genuine information rather than noise or thin-market artifacts. High correlation suggests the directional move is conviction-backed.
	 
2.4 Cross-Sectional Features
Feature
	Definition
	Formula / Pseudocode
	Normalization
	Motivation
	Cross-Sectional Return Rank
	Percentile rank of today's total intraday residual return (9:45-15:30) relative to all stocks in the universe on the same date.
	feat = rank(CumReturnResid_1530 - CumReturnResid_0945) / N  (per date)
	Ranks are bounded [0,1] by construction. No further winsorization needed. Optionally apply a normal quantile transform to map to N(0,1).
	Cross-sectional ranking removes the common market factor and focuses on relative performance. Extreme-ranked stocks (top or bottom decile) often exhibit continuation or reversal that raw return levels miss.
	Cross-Sectional Volume Rank
	Percentile rank of today's volume surprise (CumVolume_1530 / MDV_63) relative to all stocks in the universe on the same date.
	feat = rank(CumVolume_1530 / MDV_63) / N  (per date)
	Ranks are bounded [0,1] by construction. Optionally apply a normal quantile transform.
	Ranking volume surprise cross-sectionally identifies which stocks had unusually high activity relative to peers on the same day, regardless of market-wide volume conditions. High-rank stocks are more likely to experience persistent moves.
	 
Section 3: Text Feature Ideas (10-Q Filings & Earnings Call Transcripts)
The following describes two features we would create if required to use the latest available 10-Q filing and/or earnings call transcripts. We are not using this data in our model, but describe the methodology below.
Feature
	Definition
	Formula / Pseudocode
	Normalization
	Motivation
	Earnings Call Sentiment Score
	A sentiment score derived from the most recent available 10-Q earnings call transcript, computed using the Loughran-McDonald financial sentiment lexicon. Scores the tone of management's prepared remarks and Q&A separately.
	positive_words = count of L-M positive words in transcript negative_words = count of L-M negative words feat = (positive - negative) / total_word_count
	Winsorize at [1st, 99th] percentile. Z-score cross-sectionally within each fiscal quarter cohort to control for industry/era differences in tone.
	Management language in earnings calls contains forward-looking signals beyond the numbers. Negative tone predicts negative abnormal returns in subsequent days; the Q&A section is particularly informative as it reflects unscripted reactions to analyst pressure.
	10-Q Risk Factor Change Score
	A measure of how much the risk factors section of the most recent 10-Q has changed relative to the prior quarter's 10-Q, using cosine similarity of TF-IDF vectors of the risk section text.
	tfidf_curr = TF-IDF(risk_section_current_quarter) tfidf_prev = TF-IDF(risk_section_prior_quarter) feat = 1 - cosine_similarity(tfidf_curr, tfidf_prev)
	Winsorize at [1st, 99th] percentile cross-sectionally. Z-score cross-sectionally within each filing quarter cohort.
	Managers strategically expand or modify risk factor language when they anticipate material new risks. A high dissimilarity score (lots of new language) predicts negative abnormal returns in the months following the filing, as risks eventually materialize.
